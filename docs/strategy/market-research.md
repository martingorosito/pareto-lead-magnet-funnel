## 1. Three competitor lead magnets

All three are in the VA and assistant-hiring space and sell what Pareto sells: a dedicated assistant for a busy owner. I picked three different formats on purpose (ebook, calculator, quiz) so the comparison shows what each format does well and badly.

| | BELAY | Time etc | Get Staffed Up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead magnet** | "Delegate to Elevate" | "The Entrepreneur's Lifetime Value Calculator" | "Delegation Quiz: Learn Which Virtual Assistant Fits Your Law Firm" |
| **Format** | eBook (PDF download). Most downloaded on their resource hub, 11.1k+ downloads. Sister asset: "25 Things You Can Delegate to an Assistant Today" checklist (6.8k+). | Interactive calculator, 4 dropdown questions | Interactive multiple-choice quiz |
| **Promise** | "You can't keep doing everything on your own; you have to delegate. This eBook tells you how." | Find out "how much you'll earn over your lifetime, as well as the true cost of your time." Results reframe it as "your Mondays are worth..." and "the five hours you spend in your inbox every week are worth..." | Find "the ideal Offshore Assistant position that aligns with your firm's goals and challenges." |
| **Who it's for** | Any busy leader or business owner. Very broad. | Entrepreneurs in general. Broad, self-selects by profit band. | Law firm owners and solo attorneys only. Very narrow. |
| **How they capture and qualify** | Captures a mobile number plus SMS consent, nothing else. Doesn't qualify: no revenue, role or size. | Captures email after the calculation, before results. Qualifies lightly with the calculator inputs: age range, current annual profit, next year's profit target, and whether they plan to sell. | Captures first and last name, email and firm name. Qualifies strongly with firm size (Solo to 7+ attorneys), practice area (15+ options) and timeline to hire. |
| **What happens after opt-in** | SMS delivery and automated promotional messages. The page shows no call CTA or next step. | Results page with the dollar value of their time, then "Try A Virtual Assistant For Free Now" (self-serve sign-up). | Personalised role recommendation, then "SCHEDULE A MEETING WITH US" with their "Freedom Team". |

### What this tells me (and what I'm taking into my lead magnet)

1. **A PDF alone doesn't qualify anyone.** BELAY gets the most downloads with the weakest filter. Their lead list is big and mixed. Pareto has the opposite problem: it needs fewer, better founders on calls. So whatever I build must ask qualifying questions at opt-in.
2. **A number is the strongest hook.** Time etc turns "you're busy" into "your inbox costs you $X a week". That pushes the Dream Outcome side of the Value Equation without any extra effort from the visitor. I'm borrowing that idea: my audit ends with a score and an hours-per-week figure.
3. **The quiz wins on qualification and next step.** Get Staffed Up is the only one that asks size and timeline, and it's the only one that sends people to a call. That's the routing Pareto needs. Its weakness is the narrow niche (law firms only).
4. **The gap none of them fill:** a quick diagnostic for a $500K+ founder that says *what* to hand off first and *how many hours* that frees, and then routes qualified founders straight to a call. Nobody pairs the diagnostic with a personality-matched, guaranteed placement, which is Pareto's edge.
5. **Pareto's own guides don't capture leads.** paretotalent.com/guides has four strong long-form essays (Delegation Architecture, How to Delegate as a Founder, The Bottleneck, The Second Brain for Founders), all ungated. That content is good raw material for my lead magnet, and the gated, scored version is new.

## 2. Pareto's ICP, in one paragraph

> US founders who own and run a business doing at least $500K a year (Pareto's best fit sits between $500K and $5M, and the matching page speaks to "7 & 8-figure founders"), who are drowning in admin and are personally the bottleneck: the inbox, the calendar, the follow-ups and the 14 systems in their head all run through them. They've usually tried a VA or freelancer before and got burned, so they want someone vetted, trained and managed, who works full time in their time zone and sounds like them. They make the hiring decision themselves, can invest about $3,600 a month in a full-time Right Hand, and want help within the next three months.

Where each part comes from:
- **$500K floor, founder-led, US, ~2-week sales cycle:** Ivan's Second Brain intake for Pareto (Day 3 transcript). Day 2 transcript gives the range "$500,000 to $5 million" for the ideal reader.
- **"7 & 8-figure founders", "$100K+/mo", $3,600/month, $3,000 placement fee:** paretotalent.com/matching and the homepage pricing section.
- **Drowning in admin (80%), no time no life (70%), burned by a VA (60%), everything runs from memory (50%), things fall through (40%), too many hats (30%):** Day 1 slides, from Pareto's analysis of 1,500+ founder calls.
- **14 systems in their head:** Day 3 slides and Day 1 transcript.
- **"I'm the bottleneck in my own company":** Day 8 slide on client words.

## 3. Founder personas, by cause, in client words

A persona here is defined by *why* they need a Right Hand, not by age or industry (Day 8: Persona, then Pain, then Objection, then Language).

**How to read the quotes:** lines marked **[verbatim]** are exact phrases Pareto's founders said, quoted by Ivan from Pareto's call analysis or on the Wall of Love. Lines marked **[adapted]** are paraphrased from the same transcripts into first person so they're usable in copy. Never present an adapted line as a real testimonial.

### Persona 1: The Bottleneck Agency Owner
- **Cause:** The business grew faster than the owner's capacity. Every client, invoice and message routes through them.
- **Who:** Agency or service-firm owner, many clients, small team, $500K to $3M.
- **Pain, in their words:**
  - "I'm the bottleneck in my own company." [verbatim, Day 8]
  - "I'm doing everything myself." [verbatim, Day 8]
  - "I've become the full-time manager of just communications. I'm a message router." [adapted, Day 8]
  - "Whenever a client starts, I need to generate an invoice, do all of this other part." [adapted, Day 8]
- **Objection:** "It'll take me longer to explain it than to do it myself."
- **What moves them:** A list of exactly which tasks leave their plate first, and a Right Hand who takes broad goals and runs them end to end.

### Persona 2: The Burned-Before Coach, Speaker or Consultant
- **Cause:** Their personal brand *is* the business, and a previous VA didn't sound like them.
- **Who:** Coach, consultant, speaker or author. Personal brand, content, podcasts, events.
- **Pain, in their words:**
  - "I've had three different executive assistants before, and I thought it was me... burning through them." [verbatim, Eric Ritter, Wall of Love]
  - "My last VA sent AI fluff. It didn't sound like me." [adapted, Day 8]
  - "I want somebody I can meet every single day, who can speak in my voice." [adapted, Day 8]
- **Objection:** "Nobody can represent me the way I do."
- **What moves them:** Personality matching (PI Behavioral Assessment), the Second Brain that loads their voice, the Matching Guarantee ("no payment until you're excited about your match").

### Persona 3: The Founder With No Life
- **Cause:** They built the company for freedom and got a 12-hour, 7-day job instead.
- **Who:** Owner-operator, $500K to $5M, often 5 to 20 staff, still the CEO, ops manager, scheduler and firefighter.
- **Pain, in their words:**
  - "I built a company, and now I'm working in it as a job." [verbatim, Day 8]
  - "I wanted a business that would give me freedom, but instead I work 12 hours a day, every day." [verbatim, Day 8]
  - "It's only day 2 and I'm already feeling such a relief." [verbatim, Daneen Goncalves, Wall of Love: the after-state]
  - A client "was able to take vacation for the first time in two years." [verbatim story, Day 1]
- **Objection:** "I can't afford to step away right now."
- **What moves them:** Concrete hours back (Freedom 40 Guarantee: 40+ hours reclaimed in 30 days or next month free).

### Persona 4: The Dropped-Ball Founder
- **Cause:** Everything runs from memory. No systems, no SOPs, so things fall through the cracks.
- **Who:** Fast-growing founder juggling sales, ops and referrals with no one watching the open loops.
- **Pain, in their words:**
  - "I'm chasing invoices, opportunities, referral emails, and I start missing things." [adapted, Day 1]
  - "I have more than 300 open tasks and it feels overwhelming." [adapted from Ivan's own example, Day 1]
  - "My executive assistant runs point on critical projects and keeps track of endless opportunities." [verbatim, Joe Polish, Wall of Love: the after-state]
- **Objection:** "My business is too complicated to hand over."
- **What moves them:** The Second Brain build-out and a Right Hand whose job is closing loops (Day 9: "You're hired to close loops").

### Persona 5: The Established Firm That Wants Zero HR Risk
- **Cause:** They need a seat filled reliably and don't want to spend their own HR time vetting and replacing.
- **Who:** Established company, around 50 employees, structured, "everything is serious".
- **Pain, in their words:**
  - "We want somebody who's vetted, who's proven, who's managed." [adapted, Day 8]
  - "If this person is not doing a good job, we want a guarantee." [adapted, Day 8]
- **Objection:** "Offshore hires are a gamble."
- **What moves them:** Top 1% acceptance (1 in 1,000 applicants), 93% still together at 12 months, Lifetime Replacement, Pareto as employer of record.

### Which personas the funnel targets first
The lead magnet is written for **Personas 1, 3 and 4**, because the audit directly answers their cause (too much on their plate, no time, things slipping). Personas 2 and 5 are covered by the ads and the booking page, where matching and guarantees do the work.
